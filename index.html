How it works

Request an appointment, receive confirmation, verify and sign live, then receive your completed documents.

Step 1

Submit your appointment request

Appointment times are requested, not guaranteed. Every booking is reviewed and must be manually approved before it is confirmed.

Step 2

Receive your confirmation

Once your appointment is approved, you’ll receive a confirmation email requesting your booking details and the documents that require signing. Any questions you have will be answered ahead of your appointment.

Step 3

Verify your identity and sign

You’ll receive an invitation to the secure notarization platform. On the day of your appointment, have a valid, unexpired government-issued ID (state ID, driver’s license, or passport) ready and complete a brief identity verification. Payment and document delivery

Following your completed session, you’ll receive and settle the invoice. Your finalized, notarized documents will then be delivered to you.

Notarial act

Acknowledgments

The most common notarization for real estate, business, and legal documents — you don’t sign in front of me, you simply confirm that you already did.

In an acknowledgment, you appear before me online, present valid ID, and declare that the signature on the document is yours and that you signed it willingly. The document can be signed before the session — you’re acknowledging a signature, not making one during the appointment.

Because there’s no oath involved, acknowledgments are typically the fastest notarization to complete.

Common documents: deeds and real estate transfers, contracts and agreements, affidavits that call for an acknowledgment, and many business filings.

Not every document that says “acknowledgment” is automatically eligible for remote online notarization — email your document ahead of time if you’re not sure.

Notarial act

Jurats

Used when a document requires you to swear to its truthfulness — you sign live, in front of me, and take an oath or affirmation before I notarize it.

Unlike an acknowledgment, a jurat requires you to sign the document during the session itself, not beforehand. I administer a spoken oath or affirmation, you confirm the contents are true, and then sign while I witness it.

Common documents: sworn statements and affidavits, depositions, verifications, and any document with “subscribed and sworn” language.

Because the signing has to happen live, please have your document unsigned and ready to go at the start of the appointment.

Estate planning

Trust documents

Notarization for the documents that create, fund, or amend a trust as part of an estate plan.

This covers documents like revocable living trusts, trust amendments and restatements, and certifications of trust. Estate plans often involve several related documents and more than one notarial act in the same appointment.

Because trust packages vary so much in length and complexity, I quote them individually rather than at the standard single-document rate.

Not every trust or estate document qualifies for remote online notarization depending on its purpose and where it will be used. Email me your document list and I’ll confirm eligibility and pricing before you book.

Authorization document

Power of attorney

Notarization for documents that authorize someone else to act on your behalf financially, medically, or generally.

A power of attorney names an agent (sometimes called an “attorney-in-fact”) to make decisions or take action for you — anything from managing finances to healthcare decisions to a specific, limited task. Most POA documents require notarization to be accepted by banks, medical providers, and other institutions.

Common types: durable power of attorney, financial power of attorney, healthcare power of attorney, and limited or special power of attorney.

Some institutions and out-of-state uses have their own requirements for how a POA is signed or witnessed — worth confirming with the receiving party before your appointment.

Questions before booking

How much will I pay in total?

A single-document appointment during standard hours totals $35: $25 for the notarial act plus a $10 booking fee. Each additional notarial seal is $15. Witnesses provided through the platform are $10 each. Appointments outside standard hours may carry additional charges, confirmed with you before your session.

Do you provide witnesses?

Yes. Witnesses can be arranged through the notarization platform for $10 each, billed to you at cost as a non-notarial service. You are also welcome to bring your own witnesses to the session at no additional charge.

Do you handle estate planning and multi-document packages?

Yes. Packages with several documents are quoted in advance so you have a fixed number before the session. Email me your document list and the number of signers and I'll send an itemized estimate.

What are your hours?

Standard hours are Sunday through Friday, 6am to 8pm Pacific, and Saturday is closed. Additional hours are available at request, including evenings, Saturdays, and holidays. Email me with the time you need and I'll confirm availability and any additional charge before you book.

Can every document be notarized online?

No. Eligibility depends on the document type, platform requirements, and applicable law.

Do you provide legal advice?

No. E-RON WA is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice.

What do I need for the appointment?

You generally need your document, a valid government-issued ID, and a stable internet connection.

What if a document needs to be resigned?
